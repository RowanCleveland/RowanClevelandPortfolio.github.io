Engineering Project

Water Transfer System

Designing a way to move water from a well approximately 120 feet deep to a barn about 300 feet away.

The Problem

I needed to find a practical way to move water from a family well to a barn located a significant distance away. The system had to move the water reliably while dealing with the depth of the well and the length of the water line.

System Design

I worked with the idea of using a siphon-based water transfer system. The project required considering elevation, pressure, tubing, water flow, and the distance the water needed to travel.

Engineering Considerations

One of the most interesting parts of the project was realizing that the system could not simply be treated as a pump connected to a long tube. The elevation difference and friction through the tubing affected how the system could operate.

I had to think about the entire system rather than just one component. This included the water source, tubing, elevation, flow, and destination.

Testing

I tested the system in the real environment and made changes based on how the water actually behaved. Working with a physical system made it clear how different real-world conditions can be from what is expected on paper.

What I Learned

This project was one of my experiences applying engineering concepts to a problem that did not have a simple classroom answer. It taught me to consider the entire system, test assumptions, and improve a design based on real results.
