Engineering Project

Siphon Project

Designing a water transfer system to move water from a well approximately 120 feet deep to a barn about 300 feet away to provide water for irrigation.

The Problem

I needed to find a practical way to reliably move water from a well on a hill to a barn located a significant distance away without needing electricity or extensive human control. The system had to move the water reliably while dealing with the depth of the well and the distance the water had to travel.

System Design

I spent multiple days thoroughly researching siphons, understanding how they worked and making sure that I had all the data I needed to make a siphon system of my own. The project required considering elevation, pressure, tubing, water flow, and the distance the water needed to travel. I used these factors to evaluate design constraints and sketched and designed a my own system that I believed would work for the project.

Engineering Considerations

One of the most interesting parts of the project was understanding that the system couldn't just be treated as a pump connected to a long tube. Things like the elevation difference and friction through the tubing affected how the system could operate. The calculations I had to make far exceeded what I believed would be needed for a project like this.

I had to think about the entire system rather than just one component. This included the water source, tubing, elevation, flow, and the destination.

Testing

I tested the system in the real environment and made multiple material and concept changes as I worked based on how the water actually behaved. Working with a physical system revealed how different real-world conditions can be from what is expected on paper.

What I Learned

This project was one of the most impactful experiences I have had applying engineering concepts to a problem that did not have a simple classroom answer. It taught me to consider the entire system, test assumptions, and improve a design based on actual, experimental results.
